# Voice

Everything that goes out under the user's name and is read by a person: resume bullets and summary,
cover letters, screening-question answers, any free-text field on a form. The facts come from the
profile tables; this file is how they are said.

## Do not sound generated

Recruiters discard an application that reads like model output, on that basis alone, and they name
the same tells every time. The defense is not a lighter touch. It is being specific enough that no
model without the record could have written the line.

- **The generated vocabulary is banned outright.** Verbs: leveraged, spearheaded, utilized,
  orchestrated, championed, drove, streamlined, optimized, enhanced, facilitated, showcased,
  underscored, delved. Adjectives that travel with them: robust, seamless, comprehensive,
  innovative, dynamic, pivotal, crucial, significant, strategic, impactful, cutting-edge. Write the
  word someone holding that job would say out loud — used, ran, led, cut, rewrote, moved, fixed.
- **One insider noun per role or answer.** The system, form, unit, shift, queue, contract or statute
  a person in the field would recognize and an outsider would have to ask about. It is the hardest
  thing to fabricate and the strongest evidence a human wrote the line. Take it from `about`; never
  invent one.
- **Vary the shape of the sentence, not just the opening verb.** Verb + object + prepositional
  phrase on every line is the pattern recruiters name first. Open some lines on the constraint or
  the scale, let lengths differ by more than a word or two, and let sections differ in density where
  relevance differs. Uniform polish reads as generated even when every fact is true.
- **No participial tail** — "…, resulting in a 30% reduction", "…, ensuring compliance across
  teams", "…, driving adoption". An abstract consequence bolted onto a concrete clause is the most
  common generated shape there is. Put the outcome in the main clause or cut it.
- **No three-item list where the record supports two**, and **no em dash at all** — the doubled
  em-dash rate is the most measured tell of the lot, and one is enough for a reader to name the
  document. Recast on a comma, a period, or one colon per sentence at most. The dash a template
  prints between a title and its dates is the template's, not yours.
- **A number with no referent is worse than no number.** "Improved efficiency by 35%" is the
  canonical generated line: nothing says what was measured or against what.
- **Never name what was not done** — "not just…", "rather than a rewrite", "no separate system to
  adopt". State what is.

## Choose the strongest word that is exactly true

Where two words are equally honest, take the one carrying information the other lacks: **reconciled**
50,000 records rather than checked them, **negotiated** vendor pricing rather than discussed it,
**held costs flat** rather than kept them from rising. In-house shorthand and slang carry nothing the
plain word does not, and draw attention to themselves.

**Every adjective and adverb must do work.** "Roughly" hedges a real estimate and stays; "various",
"several", "multiple", "successfully" decorate and go. A modifier that can be deleted without losing
information is deleted. So does filler that survives deletion — "end to end", "results-driven",
"passionate about" — and jargon where plain English is shorter.

**No identity claims.** "Detail-oriented professional" asserts what the facts are supposed to
demonstrate. Give the fact and let the reader draw it.
