# Voice

Everything that goes out under the user's name and is read by a person: resume bullets and summary,
cover letters, screening-question answers, any free-text field on a form. The facts come from the
profile tables; this file is how they are said.

## The candidate, describing their own work to someone who could hire them

Write every line the way the candidate would say it to a peer they respect: owning the work, naming
it, and saying what it was worth. Confidence is not adjectives. It is three things the line states
outright, and a line missing one is rewritten until it has it.

- **The candidate is the actor.** "Built a service that grades answers" makes the service the
  subject; "Wrote the team's eval library" makes the candidate the subject. Sole work is claimed
  flat. Shared work names the share once ("with one other engineer") and claims the rest.
- **The thing has its name.** The system, form, unit, contract, product or statute a person in the
  field would recognise, named early and in the field's words. It is the hardest thing to fabricate
  and the strongest evidence a human wrote the line. Take it from `about`; never invent one.
- **The worth is in the sentence, not in the reader's head.** In order of strength: a measured
  result with its baseline; who depends on it and how often; what it replaced; how long it has run
  in production; what it made possible. A bullet that only says what a thing does is a product
  listing.

**The field's word for a thing is the document's word for it**, and the JD's where the JD has one.
Plain English governs everything else. "Tests that grade AI answers" for evals, or "checking the
numbers matched" for reconciliation, reads as an outsider explaining the job, and the screener
scanning for the term never finds it.

**Hedge only where the record does.** "Roughly" stays on an estimate, once. A count the record
states is stated flat. Two hedges in one sentence, or the same hedged number twice on a page, reads
as an apology.

**Where two words are equally honest, take the one carrying more information**: reconciled 50,000
records rather than checked them, negotiated vendor pricing rather than discussed it, held costs flat
rather than kept them from rising.

## Do not sound generated

Recruiters discard an application that reads like model output, on that basis alone, and they name
the same tells every time. The defense is being specific enough that no model without the record
could have written the line.

- **The generated vocabulary is banned outright.** Verbs: leveraged, spearheaded, utilized,
  orchestrated, championed, drove, streamlined, optimized, enhanced, facilitated, showcased,
  underscored, delved. Adjectives that travel with them: robust, seamless, comprehensive,
  innovative, dynamic, pivotal, crucial, significant, strategic, impactful, cutting-edge.
- **No participial tail** — "…, resulting in a 30% reduction", "…, ensuring compliance across
  teams". An abstract consequence bolted onto a concrete clause is the most common generated shape
  there is. Put the outcome in the main clause or cut it.
- **No em dash at all**, and **no three-item list where the record supports two**. Recast on a
  comma, a period, or one colon per sentence at most. The dash a template prints between a title and
  its dates is the template's, not yours.
- **Vary the shape of the sentence, not just the opening verb.** Open some lines on the thing, some
  on the scale or the constraint, some on the verb, and let lengths differ by more than a word or
  two. Varied is not rough: every line is still finished prose.
- **A number with no referent is worse than no number.** "Improved efficiency by 35%" says neither
  what was measured nor against what.
- **No identity claims** ("detail-oriented professional"), **no filler that survives deletion**
  ("end to end", "various", "successfully"), and **never name what was not done** ("not just…",
  "rather than a rewrite"). Give the fact and let the reader draw it.

## Same record, said weakly and said well

The right column adds nothing the record did not hold. It says what the left column left in the
record: the name, the owner, the scale, the term of art.

| Weak, and true | Strong, and just as true |
| -------------- | ------------------------ |
| Worked on the nursing schedule for the unit. | Run the schedule for a 60-bed unit: 45 nurses across three shifts, rebuilt each month around census. |
| Helped audit vendor invoices, which found some duplicate charges. | Recovered $40K in duplicate vendor charges by auditing three years of invoices. |
| Built a tool that checks new code for problems before it is merged. | Wrote the lint and test gate every pull request on the platform team passes before merge. |
| Set up a new system for tracking inventory that the store uses. | Set up the cycle-count schedule the store's four department leads run every Monday, replacing the annual full count. |
| Taught reading to a group of students who were behind. | Taught the Tier 2 reading intervention group, 14 third-graders, two of whom rejoined grade-level reading by spring. |
